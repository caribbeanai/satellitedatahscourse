# The Satellite Challenge

The final hour of the day. Your team picks **one** of the three challenges
below, works the notebook, and presents the answer.

Each challenge is a real question with a real number attached. Nobody
knows the exact answer before you start. You will build an honest
estimate the way a working analyst would: measure what you can measure,
research what you cannot, and say clearly which is which.

## Before you pick

Finish all four student notebooks in [`../notebooks/`](../notebooks/)
first. Every challenge draws on skills from Missions 1 to 4.

## The three challenges

Pick one. There is no bonus for doing more than one, and the presentation
at the end matters more than the code.

| | Challenge | Question | You will use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | [`Challenge_A_Storm_Bill.ipynb`](Challenge_A_Storm_Bill.ipynb) — Put a Price on Melissa's Farm Damage | What did the vegetation damage around New Hope cost, in Jamaican dollars? | Before/after NDVI composites, hectares, and public crop-price research |
| **B** | [`Challenge_B_Solar_Scout.ipynb`](Challenge_B_Solar_Scout.ipynb) — Pick the Best Site for a Solar Farm | Of three candidate sites, which should get the solar farm, and how many homes could it power? | NASA POWER sunlight records, land-cover checks, and a homes-powered estimate |
| **C** | [`Challenge_C_Green_Score.ipynb`](Challenge_C_Green_Score.ipynb) — Measure the Green Space per Person | How do two Jamaican communities compare on living green per person, against the WHO minimum of 9 m² per person? | NDVI over a bounding box, population figures, and a per-person calculation |

## How to run

Same as the student notebooks. Upload the file to
[Google Colab](https://colab.research.google.com), press play from the
top, and follow the mission.

## What to present at the end

Five minutes per team. Cover four things, in this order.

1. **The number.** Say it plainly, with units. "About 480 million Jamaican
   dollars." "Site 2, roughly 3,400 homes." "Community A: 6.2 square
   metres per person. Below the WHO minimum."
2. **How you measured it.** One or two sentences. What did the satellite
   see? What did you count?
3. **Where the number could be wrong.** Every honest estimate has bounds.
   State yours: "Our number assumes all lost vegetation was cropland,
   which it probably is not, so the true figure is likely lower."
4. **Who should act on it, and how.** A number nobody uses is a wasted
   number.

## Rules

- One challenge per team.
- Show your working in the notebook. Keep the cells you experimented
  with, do not delete them.
- If you look something up on the web (crop prices, population figures,
  the going rate for solar panels), cite the source in a markdown cell.
- Every number in the presentation must come from a cell a marker can
  re-run.
